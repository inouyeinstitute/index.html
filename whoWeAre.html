The Inouye Institute for the Arts was founded in 2007 as a rural creative arts education initiative. Its members are an intergenerational team of educators, historians, and artists who are descendants of Japanese American Confinement (JAC) survivors.

Over the past two decades, members of IIA have explored the transformative power of music, movement, art, and performance and dedicated themselves to building up the communities that welcomed and supported their grandparents after World War II. The IIA is grounded in the belief that every individual possesses inherent worth, and that cultivating compassion and self-compassion through the integrated arts is a powerful, revitalizing practice that affirms the value and dignity of every human life.

The Inouye Institute seeks to secure the history of the Japanese American internment and the need for human rights in the minds of all; train competent and compassionate teachers; and develop curricula that inspire social and emotional intelligence in generations of youth through music, movement, storytelling and visual arts.
